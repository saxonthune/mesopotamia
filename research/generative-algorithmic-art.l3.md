# L3 — Generative & Algorithmic Art (art history + theory)

The lineage of *art made by rule, not by hand* — and where Mesopotamia's
milestone 2 (a visual process created by rules) sits inside it. The organizing
axis is **rule vs. mark**: the artist authors a generative system; execution is
delegated (to a draftsman, a compiler, a CPU). CGI/rendering is a different
axis — it concerns *how pixels are computed from a model*, not *whether the
image's form came from a rule*. The two can coincide (procedural CGI) but are
not the same claim.

## Questions (the aim-directed frontier)
- Q1 Is "form generated by rule, not by hand" the defining axis of the
  generative/algorithmic-art tradition? (genealogy: conceptual art → computer art)
- Q2 Does milestone 2 — a rule-driven visual process — belong to this tradition,
  or is it "just CGI"? Distinguish *generation* (rules author the form) from
  *rendering* (graphics compute a given model's pixels).
- Q3 The authorship/ontology problem: if the rule makes the image, what is the
  art and who is the author? (the "signal/intention" position)

## Findings / annotations
- [Q1] **Confirmed.** The "rules-not-hand" axis is the tradition's spine. Genealogy
  is explicit in W2465737241: Sol LeWitt's *Paragraphs on Conceptual Art* (1967,
  "the idea becomes a machine that makes the art") and Max Bense's *Projects of
  Generative Aesthetics* (1965) are the twin manifestos; computer art is named "a
  brother of conceptual art." Same move — author the rule, delegate execution —
  whether the executor is a gallery installer (LeWitt) or a CPU.
- [Q2] **Milestone 2 IS a version of this**, on the *generation* side. The L-system
  (W2010769244), Karl Sims' evolved/procedural graphics (W2050462717), fractal
  terrain (W2029629075), and procedural texturing (W2043334098) are all "visual
  process created by rules." The user's instinct — "CGI is a rendering of graphics,
  not rules" — maps to a real, documented distinction: Gaboury's *Image Objects*
  (W3193105580) frames CGI history as *rendering a given model* (the calculating
  machine → interactive medium), which is orthogonal to whether the *form* came
  from a rule. Procedural CGI sits in the overlap; pre-rendered/hand-modeled CGI
  does not. Milestone 2 belongs with generative art because the *form is authored
  as a rule*, regardless of how its pixels are finally rasterized.
- [Q3] The "signal/intention" position (artist authors intent, machine translates)
  is operationalized by Sims (W2050462717: complexity from minimal user input) and
  by interactive evolution / Picbreeder (W2095980045, selection-as-authorship), and
  adjudicated philosophically by Danto's artworld (W4233736451) and the
  art-definition cluster (W2055423502, W2162440771). Open: none of these *settle*
  authorship; they frame it as a context judgment, not an object property.
- [open] **Coverage gaps (OpenAlex is thin on art-humanities primary texts):**
  Philip Galanter, "What is Generative Art? Complexity Theory as a Context for Art
  Theory" did NOT resolve in OpenAlex — it is the canonical *definition* paper and
  should be sourced manually (rung 3/4). Likewise LeWitt's and Bense's own essays
  exist only as secondary citations here. Early Stuttgart pioneers (Georg Nees,
  Vera Molnár) under-indexed; reach via the Nake interview (W2948937436) refs.
