## Framing notes (historiography & the abstraction trap)
- **"Mesopotamia" is a region, not a state or a people.** Greek for "between the rivers"
  (Tigris–Euphrates) ≈ modern Iraq + parts of Syria/SE Turkey/SW Iran. It spans **~3000–500
  BC** across *distinct* cultures (Sumer, Akkad, Babylonia, Assyria) and a hard **North
  (rain-fed) vs South (irrigation-dependent)** divide. Every loop/accumulator variable is
  period- and region-specific — *don't flatten 2.5 millennia into one "Mesopotamia."* The
  salinization thesis is a *southern* story; the debt edicts are mostly *Old Babylonian*.
- **The spine under Q2 / Q7.2.2 / the Graeber question is the substantivist↔formalist axis.**
  - *Substantivist* (Polanyi → Hudson → Graeber): economy embedded in temple/palace; credit
    precedes coinage; silver mainly a **unit of account** concentrated in institutional
    treasuries; debt-bondage + periodic clean-slates central.
  - *Formalist* (Morris Silver [W99391817], Steinkeller-leaning): **real markets**, silver
    that **circulated**, private enterprise.
  - Milestone-1 leans hard **substantivist** (it foregrounded Hudson by design) — a known
    bias to correct when projecting. Treat "silver hoarded in the temple/palace" as the
    substantivist *pole*, not settled fact.

### Findings / annotations
- [Q6] Strong convergence on the *scaled-household* reading: Schloen (patrimonialism)
  and W2165758805 both argue the polity is a metaphorical extension of the household,
  not a categorical break — so "are palaces big households?" → **yes** on this model
  (palace = great household, city/state = household writ large). The dissent still to
  project: corporate/communal-kinship models that posit a *real* tier between household
  and palace — W94828380 (institutional vs communal vs individual ownership) is the seed.
- [Q7] Candidate intermediate tiers surfaced: city-state (W2622796386), communal/village
  land tenure (W94828380), palatial economy as its own form (W2193152044).
- [Q7/temple] How the temple forms (W2128776836, Gelb/Diakonoff school): the temple is
  "an originally **non-economic** organization" (cultic) that *acquires* goods first, then
  land — by **absorbing community/clan ('obschina') holdings** during/before Uruk IV–III
  (Diakonoff) or when centralized-state + advanced agriculture converted clan land to
  "public land controlled by crown, temple and nobility" (Gelb). Function from the start is
  **accumulation/redistribution** "to a much larger degree than its own production." This is
  the *categorical-difference* answer (temple ≠ scaled household); the patrimonial reading
  (temple = the god's oikos) is **not yet sourced** — needs Schloen/Postgate on the temple
  specifically. Deimel's old "temple-state theory" (temple = the whole economy) is rejected.
- [Q8] Skaist (W2031483131) is the loan-contract-evolution anchor (but no abstract in
  store). The Charpin/edict refs (W1968045561, W2043009441) tie instrument evolution to
  the Q2 jubilee resets. Temple/palace *credit origins* remain Hudson grey-lit (unindexed).

### Findings — land tenure (Q7, coverage 2026-06-17)
- [Q7/tenure] Southern arable land was **not freely alienable**: W567450482 (OB real-estate
  sale docs) finds *the absence of sales of arable land in the south* — sales cluster in
  houses/other property/other regions. Points to *possession/responsibility* (institutional
  or communal allocation), not modern private ownership, for southern cereal land.
- [Q7/tenure] The three-form frame (institutional / communal / individual) is W94828380
  (Renger 1995). Its systematic breakdown is **gated**: no OpenAlex abstract, and the OA
  PDF (scholarship.kentlaw.iit.edu, article=3021) is JS-walled — curl/WebFetch get 403.
  Needs a rung-3 bridge we don't have (fulltext+condense) or an alt copy. The Gelb/Diakonoff
  context from W2128776836 partly substitutes: clan/"obschina" communal land vs temple/crown
  institutional land, the latter absorbing the former across the Uruk→ED transition.
- [open] **What owned land is used for besides cereal** (date-palm orchards, gardens,
  pasture, reed) is *not covered* — keyword seeds drifted to modern agronomy. Needs a
  dedicated seed (horticulture/date-palm/prebend tenure) or fulltext.
- [Q7.2.1] A "sale record" is a **legal cuneiform tablet** with (per W567450482) a
  *tripartite structure*, an **oath** section, and **clauses** — the load-bearing ones being
  **non-annulment / no-contest clauses**. Why record at all? The clauses are the tell: a
  record exists to make the transfer **stick and be defensible against later challenge** —
  family redemption claims, rival heirs, and crucially **reversal by edict** (andurārum could
  void land transfers). So a recorded sale with non-annulment clauses is partly an
  *anti-jubilee device*. Compl. reading from W632958447 (Postgate): writing/records are an
  instrument of **government** — the act of recording is itself a claim to legitimacy/authority.
- [Q7.2.2] *Hypothesis, not yet sourced:* many "sales" of land may be **distress sales /
  debt-foreclosures dressed as voluntary transfer** — one-sided arrangements with a *de jure*
  veneer. Circumstantial support: the no-contest clauses (you only armor a transfer that's
  contestable), the absence of southern arable sales (institutional/communal land wasn't
  freely alienable, so surviving "sales" skew toward distress), and the Q3 engrossment
  mechanism (estates absorb smallholders via debt). Confirming needs the antichresis/
  forced-sale literature — milestone-1's academia.edu "A new Old Babylonian antichretic
  pledge", Steinkeller, or Van De Mieroop — mostly **fulltext/gated**.
  - *Partial sourcing (2026-06-17):* the question is the **substantivist vs formalist** axis.
    Coercion/asymmetry side is now sourced — W2330363187: many roads *into* bondage, few out,
    and the documents carry an **elite bias** (they encode the creditor/buyer's view, so a
    "sale record" is structurally the powerful party's instrument → strengthens the *de jure
    veneer* reading). Market/equal-actor side = Morris Silver (W99391817) but its content is
    abstract-gated (stub). Still open: the actual antichretic-pledge tablets (academia.edu,
    login-walled) and Silver's argument (fulltext) — both need a rung-3 bridge we lack.

## Materialist program — method & anchors (Root C)
**Unit decision:** start from **surplus mobilization**, not surplus-value. Early Mesopotamia
has no wage-labor market, no generalized commodity production, no value-form — so *Capital*'s
opening categories don't ground here. What *does*: the **tributary / "Asiatic" mode** (Diakonoff's
two-sector model — institutional temple/palace vs communal *obschina*, which is literally our Q7),
and **staple-finance vs wealth-finance** (bulk grain/labor mobilization vs control of prestige/silver).
The staple↔wealth split *is* the loop↔accumulator split at the level of state finance.

**Method — comparative-structural, not state-by-state.** A dynasty-by-dynasty narrative catches
events but misses the forces; pure "Mesopotamia-wide" abstraction misses period/region specificity
(the abstraction trap). Bridge them: treat each polity/period as a **case of a surplus regime**, and
read the regimes *diachronically* — Uruk temple-redistribution → ED city-state competition → Akkad/
**Ur III** imperial staple-mobilization (the *bala* system; data-richest) → OB privatization/silver
(the substantivist→formalist tipping point). The *change of surplus regime* is your "change in quality."

**The three moments (= the user's goal restated):**
- start/condensation → C1 (Childe urban revolution)
- accumulations the system can bear → C2 (= Q1 salinization, Q3 concentration, Q7 two-sector)
- threshold → transformation → C3 (Tainter diminishing returns [W2304167012])
- run on the quantified substrate where it exists → C4 (Ur III labor+grain accounting)
- ecology as metabolic rift → C5 (salinization = soil-metabolism rupture)

### Findings — Root C / threshold (W2304167012 read in full, 2026-06-18)
Thompson 2004, *JWSR* — logit model, Meso 3400–1000 BC, century units, 11 vars in 4 blocs
(centralization/fragmentation · imperial size · urban pop size+growth = diminishing-returns proxy ·
7 stress/crisis: econ contraction, hinterland incursions, trade collapse, regime transition,
river levels, warming/drying, river×temp climate-interaction).
- [C3] **Headline survives:** urban pop ↔ centralization/fragmentation is significant & *negative*
  (bivariate −.006**, multivariate −.012**) — more urbanization → more eventual fragmentation,
  "precisely as Tainter predicts." This is the one robust cross-bloc link.
- [C3] **But weak where it counts:** fragmentation logit pseudo-R² = .191 (<20%); crisis logit
  = .447 — and crisis & fragmentation *don't predict each other* ("two different dynamics").
  Cross-bloc (crisis↔pop/fragmentation) links "less than systematic." Table 5: crises "rarely
  exactly coterminous" with the slow entropic decay → the linear method *under-detects lagged/
  threshold relations*. Thompson's own out: X,Y "related reciprocally but not significantly."
- [C3 → dialectic, our reading] The regression fails *in the pattern a dialectical model predicts*.
  Additive/linear/coterminous regression dissolves a developing totality into separable variables;
  the <20% residual *is* the interaction it threw away. **The tell: his one interaction term
  (river×temp) is his strongest cross-bloc finding** — proof the explanatory power lives in the
  *interaction*, not the isolated factors (= the loop-vs-accumulator thesis). "Diminishing returns"
  isn't a variable but the *form of the whole relation crossing a threshold* (quantity→quality).
  Statistics still does real work: it kills monocausal stories and Table 5 gives the *timing/
  sequence* a dialectical account needs. The right successor to a better regression is an ABM/state
  machine of the interactions themselves (cf. Lang & Ertsen W4389682420).
- [C3] **Empirical-improvement status:** the *factors* were strengthened since 2004 (high-res
  paleoclimate / 4.2-kyr event replacing Butzer's admittedly-soft "inferred" river levels;
  radiocarbon-SPD demography vs Modelski's heroic city-counts; ABM) but Thompson's *method* of
  relating them was abandoned, not improved — forward citations drift out of domain. The one
  in-domain successor is **W4295073695 "Clash of the Titans" (2022, EBA economics: empirical
  evidence vs theoretical models)** — abstract not in store; *next pull* to anchor this thread.

### Findings — Benati chapter + design-relevant extractions (book read, 2026-06-18)
W4295073695 (Benati, "Clash of the Titans", in *Ancient Economies in Comparative Perspective*,
Frangipane/Poettinger/Schefold eds., Springer 2022, pp. 111–129) is a **programmatic review**, not a
results paper — corrects the earlier "empirical successor to Thompson" framing. Its value is a
*tool/variable catalog* for the sim, not an argument.
- [C3/C4 #gamevars] Quantifiable substrate Benati enumerates (the sim's candidate state vars/signals):
  the economy is **administrative accounting** (CDLI ~322,880 texts, 176,142 administrative → core
  "document" is the ration-list/land-register, ties Q7.2.1); **climate encoded in the crop** (Riehl
  et al. 2014, δ¹³C in barley grain = drought stress measured directly); **skeletal welfare + violence
  meters** (stature/health; cranial trauma, Sołtysiak); **inequality as emergent Gini** (Bogaard et al.,
  from house-size/storage — = Lang & Ertsen W4389682420 output); **prices/wages** (Pirngruber).
- [C3 #mechanic] **NWW "Violence and Social Orders"** = ready core loop: the *natural state* suppresses
  violence by **distributing rents to an elite coalition**; surplus failure → coalition defection →
  violence → fragmentation. The loop-vs-accumulator thesis in political dress. Plus a **state-capacity**
  axis (strong/centralized vs weak/low-infrastructural) as an unused tunable.
- [method] Benati's core method ask: make **uncertainty first-class** (Monte Carlo/Bayesian; Lavan) and
  **integrate micro↔macro** (Ober) — directly the "micro grand strategy" design tension; ABM is the bridge.
- [C2/C3 #designdoc] **Kristiansen & Earle, "Modelling Modes of Production"** (same book, pp. 131+) is
  effectively a design doc: **modes as dynamic overlapping systems** (one dominant, old modes persist as
  "afterlife"); the transformation rule — *a mode dominates "until the build-up of contradictions
  activates agency and leads to a redefinition of relations of production that releases a transformation"*
  (= quantity→quality threshold as a mechanic); a **multi-scalar architecture** (agency → relations →
  mode → world-system, intra/inter-systemic reproduction; transfers = raiding/trading/migration/
  colonization) = simulation layer diagram; **economy archetypes** (Kin-ordered: Corporate/Pastoral/
  Germanic w/ **"landesque capital"** | Tributary: staple- vs wealth-finance). Center–hinterland =
  Tributary↔Pastoral interaction = UCD [W1763984623] in miniature; salinization (Q1) = decay of
  landesque capital.
