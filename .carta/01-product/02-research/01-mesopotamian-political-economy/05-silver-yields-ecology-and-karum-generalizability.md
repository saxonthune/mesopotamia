# Pass 5 — Silver, Yields, Ecology, and Karum Generalizability

> Deep-research harness, Sonnet pipeline with an Opus synthesis merge (scope/search/fetch/verify on claude-sonnet-4-6, final synthesis on claude-opus-4-8). ## Question

Ancient Mesopotamian economy and ecology (Sumer, Akkad, Babylonia, Assyria, c. 3000-500 BCE), scholarly/peer-reviewed sources only (foreground Assyriology, economic history, archaeology; EXCLUDE blogs, Medium, Substack, Wikipedia, Reddit). Seven focused questions left unresolved by prior research passes. (1) THE SILVER LIFECYCLE: how silver functioned as money: circulation vs. HOARDING, whether silver was chiefly a unit of account or also a circulating medium, the money supply and its cycling, sources and inflow of silver, the role of temple/palace as silver reservoirs, debasement/fineness, and whether silver accumulated one-way in elite/institutional hands or recirculated. (2) THE TECHNOLOGICAL RATCHET VS. REVERT: agricultural and craft technology across the long run: the ard/scratch-plough, the SEEDER-PLOUGH (the seeder attachment), threshing sledges, irrigation engineering, per-hectare and seed-to-yield RATIOS, whether technology and yields RATCHETED upward irreversibly or REVERTED across political collapses, and the quantitative yield evidence (Maekawa, Halstead, the Ur III yield figures and their reliability). (3) BIOMASS AND DEFORESTATION SPECIFICS: woodland and biomass over time: cedar and the Lebanon/Amanus forests, the Gilgamesh-Humbaba cedar narrative as ecological memory, riverine date-palm and reed ecosystems, fuel demand (baking, brick-firing, smelting), whether documented deforestation occurred and at what scale. (4) CARRYING CAPACITY: population thresholds and limits: the maximum population the alluvium could support, city ABANDONMENT and RESETTLEMENT tempo, the relationship between irrigation extent and population, settlement-survey demography (Adams surveys), and whether carrying capacity was a hard ceiling or elastic. (5) LIFE-CYCLE DEBT: household indebtedness from BRIDEWEALTH, dowry, funerary and life-cycle expenses, and whether ARREARS (unpaid tax/rent/obligation owed to palace or temple) vs. fresh consumption borrowing dominated the stock of personal debt. (6) THE WHEAT-TO-BARLEY SHIFT: whether the documented shift from wheat to salt-tolerant barley holds up QUANTITATIVELY as a salinity proxy, versus alternative explanations (POWELL critique of the Jacobsen yield/seed data, dietary/economic preference, brewing demand), and the current scholarly status of this proxy. (7) GENERALIZABILITY OF THE KARUM MODEL: whether the Old Assyrian self-governing karum / naruqqum joint-stock / bearer-note system is representative or exceptional, and how palace- and temple-ADMINISTERED trade in other periods and regions (Ur III, Babylonian, Neo-Assyrian) differed from the Old Assyrian merchant-corporation model.

## Summary

The verification pass resolved only a subset of the seven research questions, with surviving evidence concentrated on the karum/naruqqum model (Q7), the wheat-to-barley salinity proxy and Powell critique (Q6), life-cycle debt instruments (Q5), and temple institutional roles (Q1 adjacent). On the salinity proxy (Q6), the verified consensus is that wheat-to-barley shift and declining-yield data are no longer accepted as reliable salinization proxies: Powell's 1985 critique (mistranslation of textual yield data plus the alternative that barley simply outproduced wheat) has "met with significant criticism" of the Jacobsen/Adams thesis that is broadly acknowledged in current scholarship, even though salinization itself is accepted as a real, irrigation-accelerated natural process. On the karum model (Q7), the naruqqum is confirmed as a pooled, multi-investor share fund managed by a traveling merchant — but claims pushing it toward bearer-instrument transferability or characterizing it as a mass (non-elite) institution were refuted, leaving its representativeness genuinely unresolved. On life-cycle debt (Q5), the strongest verified result concerns dowry as legally distinct heritable female wealth under Hammurapi's Code rather than the arrears-vs-consumption question. The silver lifecycle (Q1), technological ratchet (Q2), deforestation (Q3), and carrying capacity (Q4) questions produced no surviving claims — every candidate on silver circulation/hoarding and on climate-driven carrying capacity was refuted.

## Findings

### [1] The wheat-to-barley crop shift and declining yield/seed data are no longer accepted as reliable proxies for progressive salinization; Powell (1985) critiqued the Jacobsen/Adams thesis on two grounds — mistranslation of key textual yield data, and the alternative that barley's dominance reflects its greater productivity rather than salt stress — and this critique is broadly acknowledged in current scholarship. Salinization itself remains accepted as a naturally occurring process accelerated by institutional irrigation intensification.

- **Confidence:** high
- **Vote:** 3-0 (three merged claims, each 1-0)
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/iraq/article/new-insights-on-the-role-of-environmental-dynamics-shaping-southern-mesopotamia-from-the-preubaid-to-the-early-islamic-period/F7084E4BF1171D8B77021B286BFE300C
  - https://faculty.washington.edu/stevehar/Paulette2012.pdf
  - Powell, M.A. 1985, 'Salt, Seed, and Yields in Sumerian Agriculture: A Critique of the Theory of Progressive Salinization,' Zeitschrift fur Assyriologie 75:7-38
- **Evidence:** Cambridge/Iraq: Powell 'criticized not only the translation of key elements of the textual data but also noted that the preference for barley in southern Mesopotamia might have been related to its greater productivity.' Paulette 2012: 'Efforts to use declining agricultural productivity and changing crop preferences as proxy indicators for increasing salinization (Jacobsen 1982; Jacobsen and Adams 1958: 1252)...have met with significant criticism (Powell 1985).' Salinization is independently confirmed as 'a naturally occurring process, but it would have been accelerated by institutional efforts to intensify irrigation agriculture.' Note: the claim that the shift specifically began in the Akkadian Period driven by salinization (attributed to Jacobsen) was REFUTED in verification, reinforcing that the proxy's chronology and causation are contested.

### [2] The naruqqum ('sack') was a pooled capital fund managed by a traveling merchant, with multiple named investors each contributing shares (8 minas of silver is an attested share size, though amounts varied), recorded by name in contract texts kept in Assur.

- **Confidence:** high
- **Vote:** 1-0
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/iraq/article/abs/partnerships-in-the-old-assyrian-trade/E0093436D4A2A3EF2A2F24CF59F6E51F
- **Evidence:** Larsen, 'Partnerships in the Old Assyrian Trade,' Iraq (Cambridge): 'The basic meaning of the word is sack...It must have denoted the bag of the travelling trader, into which his investors poured their investments.' Article cites a text with 15 investors and an 8-mina transaction; a separate 30-mina fund had shares of 1-4 minas of gold, so 8 minas was attested but not universal. Caveat: claims extending the model to bearer-instrument transferability ('write down shares...analogous to bearer instruments') and to mass participation (~2,000 Assur inhabitants, one-third in Anatolia) were both REFUTED, so the system's transferability mechanics and its representativeness vs. exceptionality remain unresolved.

### [3] Under Hammurapi's Code, the dowry was legally the woman's property that passed to her children (or, if childless, returned to her father's house / paternal relatives) on her death — never to the husband — establishing dowry as a distinct form of heritable female wealth separate from the husband's estate.

- **Confidence:** high
- **Vote:** 1-0
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/iraq/article/abs/old-babylonian-dowries/06188F56E2589F5B4ADF3DB9A816BDB5
- **Evidence:** IRAQ (Cambridge): 'If marriages dissolved without fault on the wife's part, she retained her dowry for potential remarriage. Upon her death, sons or paternal relatives inherited it—not the husband.' Confirmed against Laws 162-163: if children, the dowry goes to them; if childless, it returns to the father's house. Verifier flagged that the 'sons or paternal relatives' shorthand slightly conflates the two distinct legal scenarios but does not misrepresent the law. This addresses the life-cycle-debt question (Q5) only partially — it establishes dowry as transferred wealth, not whether dowry/bridewealth obligations drove household indebtedness, nor the arrears-vs-consumption split.

### [4] Babylonian temples were multifunctional institutions — simultaneously religious centers, law courts, schools, archive depositories, and lending/mercantile establishments — not purely religious bodies.

- **Confidence:** medium
- **Vote:** 1-0
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/abs/origin-of-banking-religious-finance-in-babylonia/B5696F26B0DD719F277A7BEDCBA9F249
  - Jursa 2010, Aspects of the Economic History of Babylonia; Kozuh 2008, Religion Compass
- **Evidence:** Quoted source (Bromberg 1942): 'The chief Mesopotamian temples [were] religious centers, law courts, schools, and archive depositories, but they were also banks and mercantile establishments.' Confidence reduced to medium because the cited primary source is 84 years old and predates modern cuneiform-archive scholarship; modern work (Jursa, Kozuh) supports multifunctionality but reframes temples as large-scale agrarian-administrative institutions with internal production rather than financial intermediaries serving external clients, and the banking/mercantile degree varied by period and temple. Two related framings — that temple finance carried national-bank-like governmental authority, and that silver served a dual unit-of-account/circulating role in temple records — were both REFUTED.

## Caveats

Four of the seven research questions produced ZERO surviving claims: Q1 (silver lifecycle — circulation vs hoarding, money supply, debasement), Q2 (technological ratchet vs revert — seeder-plough, yield ratios, Maekawa/Halstead/Ur III figures), Q3 (biomass/deforestation — cedar, Gilgamesh-Humbaba, fuel demand), and Q4 (carrying capacity — population ceilings, Adams surveys, abandonment tempo). On Q1, every silver-circulation claim was refuted, including the dual unit-of-account/circulating-medium framing for both Babylonia and the karum — so the foundational money question is effectively unanswered by verified evidence. On Q4, climate-driven carrying-capacity claims (5.2/4.2/3.2 ka events; 2000-1500 BCE demographic decline) were all refuted, leaving the hard-ceiling-vs-elastic question open. Source-quality caveats: the temple-multifunctionality claim rests on a 1942 article (Bromberg) that predates the field's archive revolution and should be re-grounded in Jursa/Kozuh/Van De Mieroop. Time-sensitivity: the salinization-proxy debate is genuinely live — current scholarship treats the Jacobsen/Adams proxy as criticized but does not unambiguously declare salinization unimportant; only the proxy's reliability is what survived verification, not a verdict on salinity's actual ecological role.

## Open Questions

- Did silver accumulate one-way in elite/institutional (temple/palace) hands or recirculate through wages and seasonal payments? Every candidate claim on silver circulation was refuted, leaving the core money-supply question unresolved.
- Was the Old Assyrian karum/naruqqum joint-stock model representative or exceptional relative to palace/temple-administered trade in Ur III, Babylonian, and Neo-Assyrian contexts? The naruqqum structure is confirmed, but its representativeness and any bearer-instrument transferability were both refuted/unverified.
- For household debt, did arrears (unpaid tax/rent owed to palace or temple) or fresh consumption/life-cycle borrowing dominate the stock of personal debt? Verification confirmed dowry as heritable female wealth but did not touch the arrears-vs-consumption composition.
- Did agricultural technology and yields ratchet upward irreversibly or revert across political collapses, and how reliable are the Ur III yield figures (Maekawa, Halstead) once Powell's critique of the underlying yield/seed data is applied? No claims on this survived.
